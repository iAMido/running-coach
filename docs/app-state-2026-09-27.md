A plain-language record of one long session: 20 commits, 99 files, about
9,900 lines. Written for the athlete and for a fresh session picking the work
up. The technical detail behind every item is in `CLAUDE.md`; this is the
"what, why, and what it means for you" version.

Everything below is **live in production** as of commit `59300d3`.

## 2. Plans are built in stages, like a real coach would

Before: one AI call read everything and wrote every week in one go.
Now (`Generate Training Plan`):

1. **Assess you** — your measured load, climbing, and which days you actually run.
2. **Understand the race** — its demand and the gap to where you are.
3. **Research** — one book search per need (climbing, descent, strength, taper…).
4. **The head coach writes the outline** — phases, every week's targets, strength programme, decisions.
5. **Phases written in parallel** by separate coaches, following the outline.
6. **Rule checks in code** — volume and climbing ramps, your training days, strength placement, recovery weeks, taper, zone labels matching their heart rates, treadmill climbing arithmetic. Broken weeks are rewritten.
7. **"Does it all fit?"** (your addition) — the head coach reads the whole plan: phase joins, recovery weeks lighter on everything, weeks serving the outline, the peak preparing you for this race. Problems are rewritten, at most twice.
8. **Save**, with a "How this plan was built" card on the plan page.

Measured on the same request: the old way produced a plan with **23 errors**
(mostly heart-rate labels that contradicted their own bpm); the staged plan had
**0**. A plan build takes ~10 minutes and costs **about $3** (it was ~$6 before
the cost work — see §5). Week 1 now always starts on a Sunday that is not
already over.

## 3. The season, phase by phase

**Season** now uses the same careful process, and the season head coach writes
a **brief for every phase**: goal, why it is that long, must-haves, don'ts,
warning signs, what it hands to the next phase, and **2–4 measurable KPIs**
(weekly km or climbing for N weeks in a row, longest run, biggest climb,
adherence, decoupling vs your own history, form, CTL).

- Each phase is built as **its own training plan when it is due** — phase 1
  straight after the season is created, the rest later, from the brief plus
  your data at that time. A September plan for March would be fiction.
- **"Due" = the last 2 weeks of a phase, or every KPI met early** (your choice).
  The app then recommends: build the next phase / advance and carry one close
  gap into the next brief / extend 2–3 weeks — never an extension that would
  push the season past race day. It only recommends; you decide.
- The **weekly review and the Saturday proposal** see the KPI table and steer
  toward the KPIs that are behind.
- The Season panel shows the current phase, its KPI table, the recommendation
  with Build / Extend / Advance buttons, and each phase's brief and dates.

## 4. Other things you can do now

- **Treadmill sessions** (Log page → select a run → *Treadmill / fix data*):
  mark it as treadmill and enter distance, duration and total climb, or work
  the climb out from grade × speed × time. Most treadmills send 0 m to the
  watch, so indoor climbing previously never counted toward your weekly
  climbing or KPIs. *Restore watch data* undoes it.
- **Add a book** (Coach Library): upload a PDF up to 50 MB (or a .txt). The app
  reads it, writes its description, methodology and tags, splits it into
  chapters and sections, adds it to the library and checks the coaches can find
  it — about 30–60 s for a typical book. *Replaces* swaps a book for a better
  version; the old one is removed only after the new one is in.
- **Coach Reports** has two tabs: *Coach reviews* and *System health* (the
  7-day Coach Health panel moved here from the home screen, with every warning
  code explained).

## 5. Models, cost and reliability

- **Models**: weekly review → Opus 5.5; chat, plan edits, plan adjust → Sonnet 5;
  Grocky → Grok 4.7; plan writers → Opus 4.7; head coach (outline, reviews,
  season) → Opus 5.5; small jobs stay on Haiku 4.5. Free models were tested for
  the small jobs and none was usable.
- **Newer models "think" before answering, and that thinking counted against
  the answer's length** — a weekly review was being cut off mid-sentence in
  production, and a streamed Opus answer could come back empty. Both fixed.
- **Plan build cost halved (~$6 → ~$3)** with quality unchanged: writers no
  longer re-read the general book text, prompts are cached, a cache warm-up
  runs before the parallel writers, and repairs rewrite only broken weeks.
- **Fallback provider**: if OpenRouter fails, every AI call automatically retries
  on NanoGPT (same models, same prices, zero-data-retention routes only). Tested
  with OpenRouter deliberately broken.

## 6. Bugs found and fixed

- **A failed plan generation deleted your current plan** — it retired the active
  plan and saved the broken output in its place. Now nothing is saved unless
  the new plan is complete.
- **Book search returned nothing** for phases with names like "Support/Build",
  so parts of plans were written with no methodology behind them.
- **Book loader scripts had been broken since May** (wrong database schema;
  pdf-parse upgrade).
- **The weekly review was being truncated** at 2,000 tokens.
- **The Saturday proposal and the scorecard assumed 12-week blocks**, putting
  anything after block 1 in the wrong season phase.
- **The Coach Library was down in production** — the PDF library crashed the
  whole page on load ("Failed to load resources").
- **Coach Health counted 93 plan-builder calls as warnings** (a label I put in
  the wrong field), and counted "no active plan" as a warning on every chat.
- **Short follow-ups in chat ("2. Don't know") got no book guidance** — the
  search only saw those words. All 6 such replies in the last week now find
  relevant books.

## 7. Mistakes made and corrected along the way

Recorded because they shaped the rules now in place:

  every reference plan and was removed; later you chose "keep hard days hard",
  which is now the rule.
- Opus 5.5 was first chosen for single-call plan building; tested, it ignored
  its thinking budget and ran out of time, so the plan writers stay on Opus 4.7.

  (dropped the speed after the comma). The head coach's review caught it.
- The first book duplicate check matched titles one way only, so a test upload
  briefly loaded *Advanced Marathoning* twice. Fixed and removed.
- The first outline took 285 s of a 300 s limit; strength exercises moved to
  their own writer and it now takes ~120 s.
